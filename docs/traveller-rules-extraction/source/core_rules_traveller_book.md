## 1. DICE MECHANICS

### 1.1 Basic Dice Roll
**Title:** 2D6 Basic Roll
**Category:** DICE_MECHANICS
**Chapter:** Core Rules
**Page:** 1
**Text:** All dice rolls in Traveller use two six-sided dice (2D6). The result is the sum of both dice, ranging from 2 to 12. The most common result is 7. Results of 8+ are generally considered successes for standard tasks.
**Notes:** The 2D6 system produces a bell curve distribution. Modifiers (DM) shift the roll result up or down before comparison.

### 1.2 Difficulty Modifiers
**Title:** Difficulty Modifiers (DM)
**Category:** DICE_MECHANICS
**Chapter:** Core Rules
**Page:** 1
**Text:** Difficulty modifiers adjust the 2D6 roll result. A positive DM (+1, +2, etc.) makes the task easier by adding to the roll. A negative DM (-1, -2, etc.) makes the task harder by subtracting from the roll. The target number remains fixed (usually 8+). Modifiers come from skills, circumstances, equipment, and other factors.
**Notes:** DM is added to the dice result, not subtracted from the target. This is the opposite of many other RPG systems.

### 1.3 Saving Throws
**Title:** Saving Throw Mechanic
**Category:** DICE_MECHANICS
**Chapter:** Core Rules
**Page:** 1
**Text:** A saving throw is a 2D6 roll against a character's Saving Throw (St) ability score. Roll equal to or under the St score to succeed. Saving throws are used to resist poison, disease, shock, and other sudden hazards. A roll of 12+1 (any roll with a 12 or higher after modifiers) always fails.
**Notes:** St is one of the seven ability scores. Higher St means better saving throws. Some hazards use modified saving throws (e.g., St-2).

## 2. CHARACTER CREATION

### 2.1 Ability Scores
**Title:** Seven Ability Scores
**Category:** CHARACTER_CREATION
**Chapter:** Character Creation
**Page:** 2
**Text:** Every character has seven ability scores, each rolled 2D6 (2-12):
- Dexterity (Dx): Agility, reflexes, hand-eye coordination
- Strength (St): Physical power, used for saving throws
- Endurance (En): Stamina, health, resistance to fatigue
- Intelligence (In): Mental acuity, learning ability
- Education (Edu): Knowledge, training, formal education
- Social Standing (Soc): Background, connections, wealth
- Appearance (App): Physical attractiveness
**Notes:** All scores are rolled independently. No rerolls in standard character creation. Scores affect career eligibility, skill acquisition, and combat performance.

### 2.2 Character Creation Process
**Title:** Character Creation Steps
**Category:** CHARACTER_CREATION
**Chapter:** Character Creation
**Page:** 2
**Text:** Character creation follows these steps:
1. Roll seven ability scores (2D6 each)
2. Choose a career (or careers)
3. Serve terms in chosen career(s)
4. Gain skills, promotions, and possibly injuries during each term
5. Retire from career with retirement benefits (wealth, skills, pensions)
6. Calculate starting credits from wealth dice
7. Purchase equipment and skills with credits
**Notes:** Characters typically serve 2-6 terms (10 years each). Multiple careers are possible. Wealth is rolled at retirement using a D6 table modified by retirement level.

### 2.3 Wealth Allocation
**Title:** Wealth Allocation System
**Category:** CHARACTER_CREATION
**Chapter:** Character Creation
**Page:** 3
**Text:** At retirement, roll D6+6 (or as specified by retirement level) for wealth units. Each unit equals 1D6 x 100 credits. Total credits are used to purchase equipment, skills, and other starting assets. Unused credits are lost. Characters can also go into debt by borrowing against future earnings.
**Notes:** Wealth tables vary by career and retirement level. A career change retirement typically gives fewer wealth dice than a standard retirement.

## 3. SKILLS

### 3.1 Skill Acquisition
**Title:** Skill Acquisition During Careers
**Category:** SKILLS
**Chapter:** Character Creation
**Page:** 3
**Text:** Skills are acquired during career service. Each term, a character may have a chance to gain a new skill based on their career and branch of service. The referee rolls 2D6 against a skill probability table. If the roll succeeds, the character gains the indicated skill. Skills are binary — a character either has a skill or does not. There are no skill levels in Classic Traveller.
**Notes:** Some skills are career-specific. General skills can be acquired in multiple careers. Education (Edu) score may modify skill acquisition chances.

### 3.3 Skill Use in Gameplay
**Title:** Skill Modifiers
**Category:** SKILLS
**Chapter:** Gameplay
**Page:** 4
**Text:** Having a relevant skill gives a +1 DM to related checks. For combat, having the appropriate Fight skill is required to use a weapon effectively. Without the skill, the character suffers a -2 DM to hit. Skills also determine eligibility for certain actions and careers.
**Notes:** Multiple relevant skills do not stack — only the most applicable skill provides its bonus.

## 4. ENCUMBRANCE

### 4.1 Encumbrance Rules
**Title:** Encumbrance System
**Category:** ENCUMBRANCE
**Chapter:** Equipment
**Page:** 5
**Text:** Encumbrance is based on Strength (St) score. A character's maximum carrying capacity in kilograms equals their St score x 10. If carrying more than St x 5 kg, the character is encumbered and suffers -1 DM to all dice rolls. If carrying more than St x 10 kg, the character is heavily encumbered and suffers -2 DM.
**Notes:** Armor and weapons count toward encumbrance. Backpacks and containers add their weight plus contents.

## 5. MELEE COMBAT

### 5.1 Melee Combat Overview
**Title:** Melee Combat System
**Category:** MELEE_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 6
**Text:** Melee combat uses the TAC (Target Attack Characteristic) system. Each weapon has a base TAC number. The attacker rolls 2D6 and adds their Fight skill bonus (+1 if skilled). If the modified roll meets or exceeds the weapon's TAC, the attack hits. The defender's armor reduces damage. Combat is resolved in segments (approximately 10 seconds each).
**Notes:** TAC values range from 4 (easy to hit) to 12 (very hard to hit). Lower TAC means easier to hit.

### 5.2 Melee Weapons
**Title:** Standard Melee Weapons
**Category:** MELEE_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 6
**Text:** Standard melee weapons and their TAC values:
- Knife: TAC 7, Damage 1D6
- Broadsword: TAC 6, Damage 1D6+1
- Cutlass: TAC 6, Damage 1D6
- Sabre: TAC 6, Damage 1D6
- Sword: TAC 7, Damage 1D6+1
- Quarterstaff: TAC 8, Damage 1D6
- Axe: TAC 7, Damage 2D6
**Notes:** Each weapon type requires a separate Fight skill. Unskilled users suffer -2 DM to hit.

### 5.3 Armor in Melee
**Title:** Melee Armor Protection
**Category:** MELEE_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 7
**Text:** Armor reduces melee damage by its Armor Class (AC) value. Common armor:
- Leather Jacket: AC 1, Cost 50cr, Weight 3kg
- Leather Armor: AC 2, Cost 100cr, Weight 5kg
- Mail Shirt: AC 3, Cost 200cr, Weight 10kg
- Flak Jacket: AC 2, Cost 300cr, Weight 8kg
- Power Armor: AC 5, Cost 10000cr, Weight 30kg
Damage is reduced by AC. If damage ≤ AC, no hit is recorded.
**Notes:** Armor does not protect against all weapon types equally. Some weapons (like energy weapons) may ignore partial armor.

## 6. FIREARMS COMBAT

### 6.1 Firearms Combat Overview
**Title:** Firearms Combat System
**Category:** FIREARMS_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 8
**Text:** Firearms combat uses the same TAC system as melee, with range modifiers. Each weapon has TAC values for different range bands: Close (1-5m), Short (6-50m), Medium (51-200m), Long (201-500m), and Very Long (500m+). The attacker rolls 2D6 + skill bonus vs. the TAC for the current range. Hits deal damage based on the weapon's damage rating.
**Notes:** Range is estimated by the referee. Cover and concealment provide additional DM.

### 6.2 Firearms List
**Title:** Standard Firearms
**Category:** FIREARMS_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 8
**Text:** Standard firearms:
- Laser Pistol: TAC 7/8/9/10/11, Damage 1D6, Range 500m, Energy
- Laser Rifle: TAC 6/7/8/9/10, Damage 1D6+1, Range 1000m, Energy
- Heavy Laser: TAC 6/7/8/9/-, Damage 2D6, Range 1000m, Energy
- Revolver: TAC 7/8/9/10/11, Damage 1D6+1, Range 200m, Ammunition
- Magnum: TAC 7/8/9/10/-, Damage 2D6, Range 200m, Ammunition
- Automatic Pistol: TAC 7/8/9/10/11, Damage 1D6+1, Range 200m, Ammunition
- Submachine Gun: TAC 6/7/8/9/10, Damage 1D6+1, Range 200m, Ammunition
- Light Machine Gun: TAC 6/7/8/9/10, Damage 2D6, Range 500m, Ammunition
- Light Rifle: TAC 6/7/8/9/10, Damage 1D6+1, Range 500m, Ammunition
- Heavy Rifle: TAC 6/7/8/9/-, Damage 2D6, Range 500m, Ammunition
- Shotgun: TAC 5/6/7/-/-, Damage 3D6, Range 50m, Ammunition

- Missile Launcher: TAC 8/9/10/11/-, Damage 4D6, Range 2000m, Ammunition
**Notes:** TAC values are listed as Close/Short/Medium/Long/Very Long. Energy weapons don't require ammunition but need power packs.

### 6.3 Armor vs. Firearms
**Title:** Firearms Armor Protection
**Category:** FIREARMS_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Combat
**Page:** 9
**Text:** Armor reduces firearm damage by its AC value. The same armor values apply as in melee combat. However, some weapons have Armor Piercing (AP) ratings that reduce the effective armor value. High-powered weapons can overwhelm light armor entirely.
**Notes:** Power armor (AC 5) provides significant protection against most personal weapons.

## 7. CAREERS

### 7.1 Career Overview
**Title:** Career System
**Category:** CAREER_NAVY
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 10
**Text:** Traveller has multiple careers a character can join. Each career has entry requirements (minimum ability scores), a skill table, a promotion table, and a wealth table. Characters serve terms of 10 years each. At the end of each term, there is a chance of being dropped, promoted, or mustering out. Characters can serve multiple careers.
**Notes:** The main careers are: Navy, Marines, Army, Merchant Service, Explorers, Scouts, Spies, and Nobles. Citizens and Drifters are alternative paths.

### 7.2 Navy Career
**Title:** Navy Career
**Category:** CAREER_NAVY
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 10
**Text:** The Navy career trains officers for starship duty. Entry requirements: St 5+, In 6+, Edu 6+. Branches: Space, Flight, General. Skills include Astrogation, Navigation, Pilot, Sensors, Gunner, Engineer, and more. Promotions advance from Ensign to Captain. Retirement benefits include ship assignments and naval pensions.
**Notes:** Navy officers are the backbone of Imperial space operations. Space branch focuses on ship command. Flight branch focuses on aerospace fighters.

### 7.3 Marines Career
**Title:** Marines Career
**Category:** CAREER_MARINES
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 11
**Text:** The Marines career trains combat troops for planetary and shipboard operations. Entry requirements: St 6+, Dx 6+, En 6+. Branches: Assault, Star Marine, General. Skills include various Fight skills, Gunner, Stealth, Recon, and Vacc Suit. Marines are elite combat troops.
**Notes:** Star Marines specialize in space combat and boarding actions. Assault Marines specialize in planetary warfare.

### 7.4 Army Career
**Title:** Army Career
**Category:** CAREER_ARMY
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 12
**Text:** The Army career trains ground combat troops. Entry requirements: St 6+, Dx 6+, En 6+. Branches: Infantry, Armor, Artillery, General. Skills include Fight skills, Drive Vehicle, Explosives, Gunner, and Leadership. Army units operate on planetary surfaces.
**Notes:** Army is the largest military career. Officers command ground forces and planetary defenses.

### 7.5 Merchant Service Career
**Title:** Merchant Service Career
**Category:** CAREER_MERCHANT
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 13
**Text:** The Merchant Service trains traders and ship operators. Entry requirements: In 6+, Soc 6+. Branches: Free Trader, Merchant Marine, Broker. Skills include Carouse, Diplomacy, Pilot, Navigation, and Jack of All Trades. Merchants profit from trade and transport.
**Notes:** Free Traders operate independently. Merchant Marines serve on company ships. Brokers facilitate trade deals.

### 7.6 Explorers Career
**Title:** Explorers Career
**Category:** CAREER_EXPLORER
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 14
**Text:** The Explorers career trains world surveyors. Entry requirements: In 7+, Edu 6+. Branches: Survey, Outfitter, General. Skills include Life Science, Physics, Space Science, Engineer, Mechanic, and Sensors. Explorers chart new worlds and report findings.
**Notes:** Survey branch conducts world surveys. Outfitter branch maintains survey equipment and ships.

### 7.7 Scouts Career
**Title:** Scouts Career
**Category:** CAREER_SCOUT
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 15
**Text:** The Scouts career trains deep-space explorers. Entry requirements: In 6+, Dx 6+. Branches: Deep, Border, General. Skills include Astrogation, Navigation, Pilot, Sensors, Stealth, and Vacc Suit. Scouts explore uncharted space and maintain scout ships.
**Notes:** Deep Scouts venture far from known space. Border Scouts patrol frontier regions.

### 7.8 Spies Career
**Title:** Spies Career
**Category:** CAREER_SPY
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 16
**Text:** The Spies career trains intelligence operatives. Entry requirements: In 7+, Soc 7+. Branches: Intelligence, Corporate, General. Skills include Carouse, Diplomacy, Jack of All Trades, Language, Stealth, and Social Science. Spies gather and disseminate intelligence.
**Notes:** Spies often use cover identities. Detection by counter-intelligence can end a career abruptly.

### 7.9 Nobles Career
**Title:** Nobles Career
**Category:** CAREER_NOBLE
**Chapter:** Careers
**Page:** 17
**Text:** The Nobles career represents aristocratic service. Entry requirements: Soc 8+. Branches: Court, Government, General. Skills include Administration, Diplomacy, Language, Leadership, and Social Science. Nobles manage estates, govern worlds, and maintain political influence.
**Notes:** Noble characters start with higher Soc scores. They have access to political and economic resources.

## 8. STARSHIP DESIGN

### 8.1 Ship Design Basics
**Title:** Ship Design Overview
**Category:** SHIP_DESIGN
**Chapter:** Starships
**Page:** 20
**Text:** Starship design is based on tonnage. Each ship has a total tonnage that determines its size and capacity. Tonnage is allocated to hull, drives (jump and maneuver), fuel, cargo, life support, crew quarters, weapons, and armor. The design process follows a specific order: hull → drives → fuel → life support → cargo → weapons → armor.
**Notes:** Ship cost is calculated from tonnage and components. A 1000-ton ship costs approximately 100 million credits base.

### 8.2 Jump Drives
**Title:** Jump Drive Ratings
**Category:** SHIP_TRAVEL
**Chapter:** Starships
**Page:** 21
**Text:** Jump drives enable faster-than-light travel. Jump drive rating (J-1, J-2, J-3, etc.) determines the maximum jump distance in parsecs. J-1 = 1 parsec, J-2 = 2 parsecs, etc. Each jump drive level costs 500cr per ton. Jump travel takes approximately 5 hours plus 1D6 hours.
**Notes:** Jump range is limited by fuel capacity and drive rating. Misjumps can occur due to navigation errors or interference.

### 8.3 Maneuver Drives
**Title:** Maneuver Drive Ratings
**Category:** SHIP_TRAVEL
**Chapter:** Starships
**Page:** 22
**Text:** Maneuver drives provide sub-light acceleration. Maneuver rating (M-1, M-2, M-3, etc.) determines acceleration in standard gravities (1G = 9.81 m/s²). M-1 = 1G, M-2 = 2G, etc. Each maneuver level costs 500cr per ton. Higher maneuver allows faster orbital operations and combat movement.
**Notes:** Fuel consumption for maneuver is significant. A ship with M-3 burns fuel rapidly at full burn.

### 8.4 Ship Combat
**Title:** Space Combat Overview
**Category:** SPACE_COMBAT
**Chapter:** Starships
**Page:** 25
**Text:** Space combat is resolved in impulse (16-minute) segments. Ships maneuver using their maneuver drives and fire weapons at opposing ships. Hit determination uses range, ship size, and Gunnery skill. Damage is allocated to ship systems based on hit location tables. Ships can be disabled or destroyed.
**Notes:** Ship combat is complex and requires tracking fuel, position, facing, and system status for each ship.

## 9. TRADE

### 9.1 Trade Computation
**Title:** Trade System
**Category:** TRADE
**Chapter:** Trade
**Page:** 30
**Text:** Trade is based on supply and demand for commodities on different worlds. Each world has trade modifiers based on its characteristics (population, government, law level, etc.). Commodities have base prices that vary by world. Profit is made by buying low and selling high.
**Notes:** The trade computation uses tables to determine supply and demand levels for each commodity on each world.

### 9.2 Commodities
**Title:** Standard Commodities
**Category:** COMMODITIES
**Chapter:** Trade
**Page:** 31
**Text:** Standard commodities include: Food, Water, Oxygen, Metals, Electronics, Pharmaceuticals, Luxury Goods, Weapons, and more. Each commodity has a base price, volume (tons per unit), and supply/demand modifiers.
**Notes:** Some commodities are illegal on certain worlds. Smuggling high-value illegal goods is risky but profitable.

## 10. WORLD GENERATION

### 10.1 Universal World Profile
**Title:** Universal World Profile (UWP)
**Category:** WORLD_GENERATION
**Chapter:** World Building
**Page:** 35
**Text:** The UWP is a standardized format for describing worlds. It consists of 10 hex digits plus modifiers:
1. Starport Class (A-F, None)
2. Planet Size (0-9)
3. Atmosphere (0-9)
4. Hydrographics (0-9%)
5. Population (0-9)
6. Government (0-9, A)
7. Law Level (0-9, None)
8. Level of Technology (0-A)
9. Population in Millions
10. World Income in Billion Credits
**Notes:** The UWP determines trade codes, world characteristics, and adventure potential.
